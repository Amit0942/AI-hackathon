## 2. Headline findings (Step 1.1–1.2)

- **14/14 tables load cleanly.** 2,279,693 total rows. Every declared primary key is unique with zero nulls in the key. Zero columns are left with unresolved (`#TODO-semantics`) meaning.
- **Table sizes vary by 6 orders of magnitude** — from `dim_slot` (6 rows) to `ridership_actuals` (2,049,632 rows, 430 MB in memory). `ridership_actuals` and `bookings` are the two tables that justify a parquet cache rather than re-reading CSV each run.
- **The inventory spine (`screens`, 11,163 rows)** splits cleanly: `location_id` XOR `vehicle_id` holds for every row (verified, not assumed) — 8,548 static screens (76.6%) vs. 2,615 mobile (23.4%). `screen_type` alone predicts this split 1:1 (`metro_station`/`bus_stop` = static, `metro_rail_coach`/`bus` = mobile), which simplifies the Phase 3 model-selection logic.
- **`dim_slot.nearest_daypart` is not a key** — "night" maps to two time blocks (1 and 6). Any daypart-level aggregation must go through `time_block_id`, never `nearest_daypart`, or it silently double-counts.
- **`bookings` mixes three different things that must not be blended:**
  - Only `booking_status == "completed"` (111,727 rows) is training data for pricing; `active`/`upcoming` (79,382 rows) is *committed occupancy*, a different input.
  - `deal_id` groups line items into a single negotiated deal (56,762 deals total). 135,624 of 191,109 lines (71%) belong to just 1,277 bundle deals (~106 lines/bundle) — summing `deal_total_value` over lines multiplies bundle revenue ~106×; must de-duplicate by `deal_id` first.
  - The real price target is `contracted_price_per_slot_per_day`, not `line_item_value` or `deal_total_value`.
- **`lost_leads` price-gap signal is usable but partial:** `price_gap_pct` is populated for only 52.4% of rows (null wherever no quote or no counter-offer exists) — this is meaningful absence (the lead died before a price existed), not missing data, and the Step 6.3 cap-calibration fit must exclude the null rows explicitly rather than imputing them.
- **Categorical vocabularies are small and closed** — 13 industry verticals, 4 campaign objectives, 13 POI types, 10 event types. These are exactly what `config/taxonomy.yaml` must enumerate, and the notebook already tests brief language against them (see §4).

## 3. Join graph findings (Step 1.3)

- **Every declared edge resolves at ≥99% integrity** (most at 100%). No broken joins were found in this dataset.
- **Two measured fan-out traps**, both requiring aggregate-before-merge:

  - `vehicles.corridor_id → route_stops.corridor_id` (up to 42 parent rows per key)

- **Cold-start preview** (full census is Step 1.7, not yet run): of 11,163 screens, those with `bookings == 0` are visible in the notebook's coverage table but the exact count/percentage was not captured into this handoff — re-run the notebook to pull the number before starting Phase 6 fallback-ladder design.

## 4. Campaign brief parsing (relevant to Step 1.8)

- All 6 `.docx` briefs in `Data Sets/Campaigns/` parse deterministically (no LLM) via `briefs.py`, extracting `word/document.xml` directly rather than using `python-docx` — required because the briefs put header fields in a Word *table*, which `python-docx` would otherwise separate from body paragraphs and destroy label/value adjacency.
- Two layers, kept deliberately separate: `parse_brief` (lossless structure — title, header fields, numbered sections, RFP requirement list) and `derive_fields` (regex-normalised budget, duration, age band, slot request).
- The notebook cross-checks brief vocabulary (industry vertical, objective) against the real data vocabulary (`bookings.industry_vertical`, `campaign_objective`) — any brief term that doesn't bind becomes a required `config/taxonomy.yaml` synonym, not a bug.
- Everything the regexes can't bind (named venue types, walking-radius limits, weekend weighting, creative-format restrictions) is preserved verbatim as `unresolved_requirements` rather than silently dropped — this list is the Phase 4/5 capability checklist.
- These parses are meant to serve as the **gold-parse fixtures** for the Phase 4 LLM extractor.

## 4a. Inventory shape (Step 1.4)

- **As-of date is `2026-08-19`**, triangulated exactly from four independent signals (last `completed` end date, first `upcoming` start date, the `active` span, ridership actuals cutoff). Every train/test split and availability check must use this date, not the wall clock.
- **Capacity is 6 rotation slots per screen×block, measured then proved** — `rotation_type` is a label on `slots_booked_per_day`, not independent information, and a sweep line over all 191,109 bookings confirms 6 as a real ceiling (0 units ever sold beyond it).
- **Sellable supply:** 401,868 slot-units/day network-wide, 75,149,316 over the 187-day forward horizon, 13.9% already committed. But this average is misleading — commute-peak blocks run 4–5x the utilization of night blocks (§3.3), so availability must be checked at `screen × block × date` grain, never at screen level or against a network average.
- **Static/mobile splits 1:1 on `screen_type`** (metro_station/bus_stop = static, 76.6%; metro_rail_coach/bus = mobile, 23.4%), so no null-checking is needed to pick the D1 exposure model.
- **Concentration is the overlap problem D4 must solve:** bus stops carry exactly 3 screens each, but metro stations carry a median of 34 and up to 50 — fifty screens in one station is one audience cluster, not fifty independent reach buys.
- **Price per slot falls as slot count rises** (up to ~7% cheaper at 6 slots vs. 1), first evidence for the non-linear impressions/pricing nuance the problem statement calls out — Step 1.5 tests whether this survives confounding.
- **Cold-start is concentrated, not scattered:** 53.9% of ACS (value-tier city) screens have zero booking history vs. 9.2% DAT and 0.0% LH; concentrates in the cheapest static formats. This is the population the fallback ladder must serve.
- **Capability gaps vs. the briefs** (§5 of the doc): no digital/static flag, no aspect-ratio column, no slot-duration constant for the briefs' "seconds per minute" language — each needs a stated assumption or config constant (`config/taxonomy.yaml`) before Phase 4/5, not a demo-day surprise. "Bus-rear" and "metro platform" requests ARE directly supported (`screen_type` + `position`).

## 4b. Demand & pricing profile (Step 1.5)

- **Only `completed` bookings (111,727 lines) are safe training data.** `active`/`upcoming` (79,382 lines) is committed occupancy, a different input — never blend the two.
- **Ranked realised price drivers:** `time_block_id` and `screen_size` have the largest spread, `city_id`/`market_tier` tie exactly (1:1 mapped), `rotation_type` is an order of magnitude weaker (it's a slot-count label, not a real driver). `screen_size`/`screen_type`/`city_id` are collinear — Phase 6 must check joint effects, not just fit single-feature coefficients.
- **The de-dup trap is bigger than "lines per deal" suggests:** naively summing `deal_total_value` over line rows overstates network booked value by **329x** ($805.8B vs. the correct $2.45B), not the ~106x that "lines per deal" would imply, because bundle value is concentrated in bundles that are both high-value and high-line-count. **Any revenue rollup anywhere in the system must group and de-duplicate by `deal_id` first.**
- **Bundles price ~9.5% below standalone at the median** — bundle pricing must be its own artifact (Phase 7.3), not an average of line prices.
- **The slot-count discount is real but inverts by screen type** — this is flagged as the single most important finding in the step. `bus` shows a consistent discount, `metro_station` shows it on peak/midday blocks only, `metro_rail_coach` shows almost no effect, and **`bus_stop` price per slot rises with more slots booked** (opposite sign). A network-wide slot-discount curve gets `bus_stop` backwards; the base-rate model needs a `screen_type × slot_count` interaction term.
- **Empirical price-cap calibration:** deals survive to verbal-agreement-or-later 38–48% of the time up to a 15% gap over client target, then collapse to single digits above ~15–20%. This is a measured willingness-to-pay ceiling, not an invented guardrail — directly answers the problem statement's "no one knows if the price is right."
- **Competitor-named leads lose at a much thinner price gap** (3.4% vs. 22.9% median) — competitive pressure should lower the effective cap as its own signal, not just correlate with a bigger gap.
- **Lead-to-loss cycle length scales with sales stage** (4 days at inquiry → 39.5 days at contract-sent) — gives the recency-decay half-life a concrete, measured scale instead of an arbitrary one.

## 4c. Context profile (Step 1.6)

- **POI `scale` (neighborhood/minor/major/flagship) is NOT an ordinal footfall proxy** — `minor` sits below `neighborhood` in median footfall. Must be modelled as an unordered categorical; `est_daily_footfall` does the magnitude work.
- **Validated POI-proximity radius is 0.3–0.5 km.** Coverage keeps rising past that, but it's a selection effect (large anchors get logged as "nearest POI" even a kilometre out) — most gain beyond 0.5 km is duplicate coverage, not new signal.
- **`side_of_road` is not a distance proxy** (near-side and far-side POIs sit at nearly identical median distances) — keep as an independent visibility term in the D1 model.
- **Events cluster on already-scarce inventory:** `evening` is the dominant event daypart (129 of 367 events), which lands on time block 5 — already the highest-median-price block per 1.4 §4.1. Event surges stack on premium inventory rather than filling slack capacity.
- **`dominant_occupation` is the sharpest zone discriminator** (median income_index 154.3 white_collar vs. 78.4 student) — stronger signal than any single demographic percentage.
- **Always use `ridership_actuals`, never `estimated_ridership`** — the schedule is directionally wrong in *opposite* ways for weekday (+7.6% actual) vs. weekend (−13.3% actual), so no single correction factor works for both.
- **Weekday and weekend ridership peak on genuinely different blocks** (weekday: block 5, evening; weekend: block 4, midday) — a single reused curve would misplace the weekend peak by a full block. Time block 1 (00:00–04:00) has zero ridership rows anywhere (no overnight service) and needs a POI-only or stated-flat fallback for any exposure model.

## 4d. DQ register & cold-start census (Step 1.7)

- **The dataset is unusually clean:** 0 exact duplicates, 100.00% referential integrity with 0 orphans across all 14 measured joins (including the two new context-table joins), 0 impossible-value violations, across all 14 tables.
- **Every null is meaningful, none need imputation** — e.g. `lost_leads.client_target_price_per_slot_per_day` (47.6% null) means no counter-offer was ever made; imputing it would fabricate a negotiation that never happened.
- **Cold-start is a pure commercial-history problem, not a context/audience-data gap** — every static screen has a POI in range and every mobile screen has ridership coverage (0.0% gap on both). D1's exposure models can run on any screen; only D3's pricing model needs the fallback ladder.
- **11.0% of all screens (1,224 of 11,163) have zero booking history**, plus a further 1.3% with only 1–4 lines. The gap concentrates by screen type too: `bus_stop` 37.5%, `bus` 26.5%, vs. `metro_station` 1.5% and `metro_rail_coach` 0.0%.
- **The cold-start population is coherent, not scattered** — it's specifically the value-tier city's cheaper static inventory. This is what makes a cohort-based fallback ladder (peer screens on the same corridor/location → zone × type × position × size cohort → city × type baseline) credible rather than a patch, and it's the honest answer to "how would this scale to a new city."
- **Local-time assumption flagged as unverified**: every timestamp is a bare local value with no UTC offset; "all times are local to their row's city" must be carried into the domain model as a stated assumption, not silently relied upon.

## 4e. Domain model, repositories, C4, and acceptance tests (Phase 2)

- **Every domain invariant is enforced at construction, not trusted to the caller.** `Screen` rejects a row with both/neither of `location_id`/`vehicle_id`, or where `screen_type.is_static` disagrees with that split (Step 1.4 §2.1's measured 1:1 match). `PriceQuote` rejects `floor > target`, `target > cap`, or a `recommended` price outside `[floor, cap]` — the Step 6 exit-criterion property test is now a type invariant, not something that could be forgotten in a later engine. `ReachEstimate` rejects `unique_reach > gross_impressions` — de-duplication cannot manufacture audience. All three were unit-tested to actually raise (`tests/test_domain.py`), not just declared.
- **`InMemoryRepositories` re-validated the Phase 1 findings as a side effect.** Loading all 11,163 screens through `Screen` end-to-end produced zero validation errors and reproduced Step 1.4's exact counts (8,548 static / 2,615 mobile; per-type counts all match). This is independent confirmation of Step 1.4 §2.1, not just a repetition of it.
- **A real off-by-one bug was caught and fixed during this work:** the initial `compute_as_of_date()` returned `2026-08-18` (the max `completed` end date itself) instead of `2026-08-19`. Step 1.4 §1.1 is explicit that the as-of date is the day *after* the last `completed` end date (one full day of separation from the first `upcoming` start), so the fix adds `+ timedelta(days=1)`. Now covered by a regression test (`test_as_of_date_matches_step_1_4`) asserting the exact date, and by `test_compute_as_of_date_is_one_day_after_last_completed_end`.
- **`Confidence.merge_confidence()` always returns the weakest input**, never an average — a `Package`'s confidence is the weakest-link merge of its lines' price-quote confidences. This is deliberate: a bundle should never look more trustworthy than its least-trustworthy component just because the other components are strong.
- **The six real briefs were read by hand for Step 2.5**, independently of `agentiq.data.briefs`'s regex output, so a bug in the deterministic parser can't also be baked into its own test fixture. Confirmed: `config/taxonomy.yaml`'s `environment_types` list was in fact built to match these briefs' location-requirement language 1:1 (e.g. brief 1's "auto-retail arterial corridors" → `auto_retail_arterial_corridor`) — the taxonomy and the briefs are already mutually consistent, which Phase 4/5 can rely on rather than re-derive.

## 7. Recommended build order for Phase 3+

Given the judging criteria (soundness of ML/OR/agentic integration, explainability, performance/scalability) and what Steps 1.4–1.7 already de-risked — with Phase 2 now supplying the contracts (domain types + repository protocols) every engine below should be written directly against, rather than each engine inventing its own shapes:

1. **D1 Audience Profile Engine** first — smallest thing that unblocks D2 and D4. Step 1.6 supplies every parameter needed (0.3–0.5 km POI radius, `scale` as unordered categorical, `side_of_road` as an independent visibility term, per-daypart weekday/weekend curves, POI-only fallback for time block 1).
2. **D3 Demand Forecasting & Pricing** in parallel — no dependency on D1. Step 1.5 already provides the ranked driver list, the `screen_type × slot_count` interaction requirement, the empirical price-gap survival curve for cap calibration, and the competitor/recency signals for the demand-pressure model. Step 1.7's cold-start cohort structure (corridor/location peers → zone×type×position×size → city×type) is ready to implement directly.
3. **D2 Campaign↔Screen Relevance Scorer** next — thin scorer layered on D1's output plus the brief-parsing infrastructure already built (§4).
4. **D4 Impressions Optimizer** — Step 1.4 §3 already sized the real problem (post-eligibility-filter decision pairs number in the low thousands, not tens of millions), so a greedy/submodular default with an exact-ILP "thorough" mode is viable and can cite measured latency/quality tradeoffs rather than a hypothetical.
5. **D5 Agentic Orchestration** last — per ADR-0001 it must be a planner-executor over typed tools, not a free-form chat loop, and it's a thin wrapper over 1–4 rather than a large build in its own right.

Rationale: pricing and optimization are where Steps 1.4–1.5 uncovered findings (the `bus_stop` slot-discount inversion, the 329x bundle de-dup trap, the empirical price-cap curve, the exact optimization problem sizing) that most teams building from the raw CSVs won't have — building there first converts already-completed research into scored criteria fastest.

## 8. D3 build kickoff (2026-08-21) — decisions logged before writing code

Per solution_plan.md Phase 6, building D3 as a thin vertical slice first. Decisions made with the user before implementation, so they're recorded rather than assumed:

- **Scope for this pass:** Step 6.1 (demand index) + 6.3 (floor/target/cap band) + 6.5 (cold-start ladder) only. Steps 6.2 (footfall forecast), 6.4 (win-probability recommended price), and 6.6 (human overrides) are deferred to a fast-follow pass, not skipped. Rationale: land a working, testable `PriceQuote` end-to-end before adding the forecast and optimizer layers on top.
- **Base-rate model (6.3):** regularised linear (ElasticNet/Ridge) over `city_id, screen_type, screen_size, time_block_id, rotation_type, slots_booked_per_day`, chosen over GBM+SHAP or a pure stratified-median lookup for interpretability — each dollar must be attributable to a `Contribution` per the `Explanation` contract, and 1.5's findings don't show nonlinearity severe enough to justify GBM's extra complexity/dependency cost.
- **screen_type × slot-count interaction (6.3):** encoded as full categorical interaction terms (one-hot `screen_type` × continuous `slots_booked_per_day` product terms), not four separate per-screen-type models — matches 1.5 §5.2's 24-cell slope table directly and keeps shared-sample efficiency on the other coefficients (city_id, time_block_id, etc.) across screen types.
- **Win-probability model (6.4, deferred):** when built, will be a logistic regression on `price_gap_pct` (+ `competitor_mentioned` per 1.5 §6.5, + client tier), fit on `lost_leads` outcomes — not a step-function lookup off the six §6.2 buckets — because the recommended-price step needs a smoothly optimizable curve for the argmax, and logistic regression tracks the empirical survival curve closely enough to trust.
- **Price-gap definition at generation time (6.4, deferred):** `gap = (recommended - client_target_price) / client_target_price`, matching `lost_leads.price_gap_pct`'s own definition exactly. This means `PriceQuote`/win-probability generation will need a `client_target_price` input (from brief or client history/tier) — **this is a new dependency to design when 6.4 is built**, since no such input exists yet in the repository protocols.
- **`segment_heat` / industry-vertical dependency (6.1):** since D2/brief-intake (which would supply a `CampaignBrief.industry_vertical`) doesn't exist yet, `DemandSignal` computation takes an **optional** `IndustryVertical` parameter; `segment_heat` defaults to a neutral `1.0` multiplier when `None` is passed. This keeps D3 independently buildable and testable today, and slots into D5 orchestration later without a signature change — chosen over hardcoding `segment_heat=1.0` unconditionally, which would require revisiting the function signature later.
- **Step 6.6 (human overrides):** deferred entirely until D6/API exists. `PriceQuote.human_overrides` is already a field on the domain type (Phase 2); the *consumption* logic (a pure function that adjusts the band given overrides) will be built when 6.6 is picked up, but no API endpoint or UI stub is being created now since D6 isn't scaffolded.

**Next:** implement `src/agentiq/pricing/` — demand index (6.1), base-rate model + price band (6.3), cold-start ladder (6.5) — against `data/repositories.py` protocols and the `DemandSignal`/`PriceQuote` domain types, backed by unit tests asserting the invariants (`floor <= target <= cap`, monotonicity in demand, every price cites its ladder rung) per Phase 6 exit criteria.

## 10. D1 build result (2026-08-22) — Steps 3.1/3.2/3.3(fallback)/3.4/3.5 implemented

Ridership is aggregated in two stages to avoid exploding the 2M-row `ridership_actuals` table through a per-stop join: collapse to `(route_id, day_type, time_block_id, date)` daily totals first, average over dates, *then* distribute that small table across the stops each route serves via `route_stops`. Exposes per-location and per-corridor ridership shares/totals and trip frequency, all precomputed once at construction (~17s for the full network). Also added `bucket_time_to_block()`, the `HH:MM` → `dim_slot.time_block_id` mapping D1 needs and no repository previously provided.
- **`AudienceProfile` domain type gained an `explanation: Explanation` field** (`domain/inventory.py`) — it didn't have one despite CLAUDE.md's "every scored/ranked output carries an Explanation" rule; safe to add since nothing constructed this type yet (D1 didn't exist). No other domain type touched.
- **`src/agentiq/audience/static.py`** (Step 3.1) — resident/zone base (`resident_population × daytime_population_multiplier`) + transit throughput (ridership at the stop) + POI pull (0.3-0.5km radius, footfall-capped, side-of-road and peak-daypart weighted, per Step 1.6 §2-3) + a position/size visibility modifier, distributed across the six time blocks using the location's own measured ridership curve (falling back to the Step 1.6 network-default curve only when a location has zero ridership rows). `market_tier` is read from `cities.market_tier` directly — an earlier draft wrongly derived it from `zone_demographics.income_index`, caught before commit.
- **`src/agentiq/audience/mobile.py`** (Step 3.2) — interior screens (`metro_rail_coach` panels, no `position` — Step 1.1's proven invariant) get full corridor ridership as captive-dwell audience; exterior screens (`bus` left/right/top/back) get the average POI/resident pull across the corridor's stops, discounted for a passer-by glimpse. Per-corridor exterior-pull computation is cached (`CorridorExteriorCache`) since many vehicles/screens share one of only 94 corridors — recomputing per screen would have re-run the same POI queries thousands of times.
- **`src/agentiq/audience/semantic.py`** (Step 3.3) — **deterministic rule-based fallback, not an LLM call** — no LLM endpoint is configured in this environment. Grounds labels in real `poi_type` → `environment_type` mappings, `zone_name` (`financial_district_node`), and network-hub adjacency (`central_metro_entry`); `airport_transit_corridor`, `auto_retail_arterial_corridor`, and `mall_beauty_retail_entry` are never assigned because no `poi_type` in this dataset's 13-value vocabulary grounds them — dropped, not guessed, matching the brief-parser's "record as unresolved" convention. Swapping in a real LLM agent later means replacing this module's internals; the controlled-vocabulary guardrail and evidence requirement stay identical.
- **`src/agentiq/audience/overlap.py`** (Step 3.4) — sparse `{(screen_id_a, screen_id_b): coefficient}` graph from four signals: same location (1.0), same vehicle (1.0), same corridor/different vehicle (0.4, a stated assumption — no direct measurement of cross-vehicle audience overlap exists in this dataset), and shared POI catchment across different locations (Jaccard ≥ 0.3). An inverted `poi_id → [location_id, ...]` index keeps this to comparisons between locations that actually share a POI, not all `C(910, 2)` pairs.
- **`src/agentiq/audience/reach.py`** (Step 3.5) — `impressions = exposure × slots^alpha` (0 < alpha < 1, strictly concave, proves "doubling slots never doubles impressions" by construction) and `unique_reach = scale × (1 − e^(−effective_exposure/scale))` (concave, zero at zero, so sub-additivity — `reach(a+b) ≤ reach(a)+reach(b)` — holds by construction too, not just on sampled inputs). Two limitations are stated in the `Explanation.fallbacks_used` rather than hidden: the interval assumes independent days (no autocorrelation modelling), and no future holiday calendar exists in the raw data so a forecast date is never treated as a holiday even inside a window that covers one.
- **`src/agentiq/pricing/overrides.py`** (Step 6.6) — `apply_human_overrides()`: a pure function taking an already-built `PriceQuote` plus the three `config/pricing.yaml`-committed override fields (`expected_footfall`, `strategic_discount_intent`, `competitive_intel`), returning a new `PriceQuote` (frozen `model_copy`, original untouched) with the adjustment logged in `human_overrides` and explained.
