## 2. Nulls — every one is meaningful, not missing

| Table.Column | Null % | Why it's null | Handling rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `events.poi_id` | 23.4% | Street events/parades have no host POI | Anchor to `anchor_location_id` only; never impute a POI |
| `screens.vehicle_id` | 76.6% | Static screens have no vehicle | Null is the static/mobile discriminator itself |
| `screens.location_id` | 23.4% | Mobile screens have no fixed location | `location_id` XOR `vehicle_id` is exact (re-verified here, matches Step 1.3) |
| `screens.position` | 12.5% | `metro_rail_coach` interior panels have no mount face | Structural null (confirmed in Step 1.4 §2.3), not a gap |
| `lost_leads.company_name_raw` | 55.7% | Populated only for the 643 leads with no `client_id` | Complementary with `client_id` — verified exactly one of the two is always populated, on all 1,450 rows |
| `lost_leads.client_target_price_per_slot_per_day` | 47.6% | No counter-offer was ever given | Exclude, don't impute, when fitting the price-gap curve |
| `lost_leads.price_gap_pct` | 47.6% | Derived from the field above | Same rule |
| `lost_leads.client_id` | 44.3% | Lead came from a new prospect | Use `company_name_raw` as the identity fallback |
| `lost_leads.quoted_price_per_slot_per_day` | 36.6% | Lead died before a quote was sent (`initial_inquiry` stage) | Same exclusion rule |

Every null-bearing column's missingness is itself a fact about the row.
None require imputation; several would be actively wrong to impute (e.g.
filling `client_target_price_per_slot_per_day` would fabricate a
counter-offer that was never made).

## 3. Referential integrity, duplicates, and impossible values

All 14 measured joins — including the two new context-table joins Step 1.3
didn't cover (`points_of_interest.anchor_location_id`,
`events.anchor_location_id` / `events.poi_id`) — resolve at **100.00%
integrity with zero orphans**. Zero exact duplicate rows in any of the 14
tables.

Every impossible-value check returns zero violations:

- `bookings.duration_days` matches `end_date − start_date + 1` on all
  191,109 lines, exactly.
- No booking has an inverted date range or a `booked_date` after its own
  flight `start_date`.
- No `slots_booked_per_day`, `contracted_price_per_slot_per_day`,
  `line_item_value`, `price_gap_pct`, or `negotiation_rounds` falls outside
  its valid range.
- `zone_demographics`' four age bands sum to exactly 100.0 on all 30 zones
  (std ≈ 3.7e-15 — floating-point noise, not a real deviation).
- The km/mile distance pair on every POI row agrees to within 0.0005 km —
  rounding only.

**Timezone/date-format note.** Each city has a distinct IANA timezone, but
no raw date/time field carries an explicit UTC offset — every timestamp is a
bare local value. "All times are local to their row's city" is a documented
**assumption**, not something the data itself proves, and should be carried
into the Phase 2 domain model as such rather than silently relied upon.

## 4. Cold-start census

Every static screen has at least one POI within range, and every mobile
screen's corridor has ridership coverage (0.0% gap on both). **The only
signal gap in this dataset is booking history.** Cold-start is therefore a
pure *commercial-history* problem, not a context/audience-data problem —
D1's exposure models can run on any screen regardless of booking history,
but D3's pricing model cannot. This is exactly why the plan scopes the
fallback ladder to Phase 6, and confirms that scoping is correct.

| facet | value | screens | with_history | no_history | cold_share |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| city_id | ACS | 1,736 | 800 | 936 | 53.9% |
| city_id | DAT | 3,123 | 2,835 | 288 | 9.2% |
| city_id | LH | 6,304 | 6,304 | 0 | 0.0% |
| screen_type | bus | 1,215 | 893 | 322 | 26.5% |
| screen_type | bus_stop | 2,157 | 1,348 | 809 | 37.5% |
| screen_type | metro_rail_coach | 1,400 | 1,400 | 0 | 0.0% |
| screen_type | metro_station | 6,391 | 6,298 | 93 | 1.5% |

11.0% of all screens (1,224 of 11,163) have zero booking history
network-wide, plus a further 1.3% (144 screens) with only 1–4 lines — thin
enough that a per-screen price model would still be unreliable there too.
This re-confirms Step 1.4 §4.4's finding (53.9% ACS cold-share here vs 53.9%
there — exact match) and sharpens it by screen type: the gap concentrates
in the cheaper static formats (`bus_stop` 37.5%, `bus` 26.5%) while
`metro_station` (1.5%) and `metro_rail_coach` (0.0%) are essentially fully
covered.

**The cold-start population is exactly the value-tier city's cheaper static
inventory** — a coherent, addressable population, not scattered one-off
gaps. That coherence is what makes the fallback ladder's cohort-based rungs
(peer screens on the same corridor/location, then zone × type × position ×
size cohort, then city × type baseline) a credible design rather than a
patch: a screen with no history in ACS looks like dozens of other ACS
`bus_stop` screens, not like an isolated anomaly.
