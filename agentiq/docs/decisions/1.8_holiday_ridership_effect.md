# Step 1.8 (carried into 6.2) — Holiday ridership effect

> Measured against the real `ridership_actuals` table (2,049,632 rows; 19,838
> holiday rows, ~0.97%), joined to `route_schedules` -> `route_stops` ->
> `locations` -> `zone_demographics`, and separately to `points_of_interest`
> (nearest POI within the Step 1.6 §3 validated 0.3-0.5km radius).

**Purpose.** Close Step 6.2's previously-stated gap ("no future holiday
calendar exists in the raw data, so a forecast date is never treated as a
holiday"). Two questions: (1) how much does ridership actually change on a
holiday, and (2) does that change differ by the kind of area a location
sits in (the hypothesis being tested: malls see a footfall uplift on
holidays, offices see a drop).

## 1. Headline number

**Holiday ridership is 0.5433x non-holiday ridership, network-wide** — a
mean of 97.98 riders/trip on holiday rows vs. 180.35 on non-holiday rows.
This is the single multiplier now used by `pricing/holidays.py` /
`pricing/footfall.py`'s Step 6.2 forecast.

## 2. Does the effect differ by area type? No.

Grouped by the dominant POI type within 0.5km of each location (26.3M of
27.6M ridership-join rows had one):

| dominant nearby POI type | non-holiday mean | holiday mean | ratio |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| government_building | 189.80 | 104.43 | 0.550 |
| tourist_landmark | 253.98 | 138.84 | 0.547 |
| entertainment_district | 206.78 | 112.70 | 0.545 |
| shopping_mall | 196.04 | 106.79 | 0.545 |
| hotel_convention | 205.44 | 111.76 | 0.544 |
| grocery_anchor | 207.13 | 112.64 | 0.544 |
| residential_tower | 194.47 | 105.60 | 0.543 |
| hospital | 197.68 | 107.17 | 0.542 |
| museum | 200.47 | 108.58 | 0.542 |
| office_park | 185.28 | 100.34 | 0.542 |
| university | 190.74 | 102.98 | 0.540 |
| corporate_campus | 142.50 | 76.93 | 0.540 |

Every category — `shopping_mall` (the "high footfall on holidays"
hypothesis) and `office_park`/`corporate_campus` (the "low footfall"
hypothesis) alike — lands in a **1-point-wide band, 0.540-0.550**. Re-run
grouped by zone `dominant_occupation` instead of POI type (blue_collar,
mixed, retail_service, student, white_collar; 20,568-103,903 rows per
category, so not a thin-sample artifact): same result, 0.540-0.550.

**Conclusion: the malls-up/offices-down hypothesis is not supported by
this data.** Transit ridership drops by essentially the same ~46% on a
holiday everywhere in the network, regardless of what's nearby. This makes
sense once stated plainly: `ridership_actuals` measures *transit* usage,
which is overwhelmingly commute-driven; a holiday's dominant effect is
"fewer people going to work/school," and that effect swamps whatever
smaller, POI-type-specific footfall shift might exist at any single
location. A POI-type-differentiated multiplier would be inventing precision
this measurement does not support — the honest model is one global
constant.

**What this does *not* rule out:** a real difference in foot traffic
*at the POI itself* (as opposed to transit ridership near it) — but
`points_of_interest.est_daily_footfall` is a single static estimate with no
holiday-specific figure anywhere in the raw data, so that question is
unmeasurable from this dataset, not merely un-modelled here. Flagged as a
capability gap, not silently assumed away.
