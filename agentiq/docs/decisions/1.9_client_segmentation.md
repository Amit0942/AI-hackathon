# Step 1.9 (carried into 6/D3) — Client segmentation

> Measured against `client_facts` (520 rows) joined to `bookings`
> (`client_id` resolves 100%) — `docs/data_dictionary.md`.

**Purpose.** The user asked for clients to be segmented into a few types
(reach-centric, "result"/conversion-centric, frequency-centric, budget
types), and asked directly whether this needs a separate model or the data
doesn't support one. It supports a rule-based classifier — no clustering
model was built, and here is why.

## 1. The requested segments already exist almost verbatim in the data

`bookings.campaign_objective` has exactly four values — `awareness`,
`conversion`, `frequency`, `reach` — and `bookings.client_id` resolves 100%
to `client_facts`. A client's dominant historical objective (the mode of
`campaign_objective` over their settled bookings) *is* the requested
segmentation, measured rather than inferred:

| segment (by dominant objective) | client count |
| --- | --- |
| awareness | 296 |
| conversion | 83 |
| reach | 81 |
| frequency | 40 |
| none (no settled history yet) | 20 |

`client_facts.budget_variance_pct` gives a second, independent axis:
clients at/below the network median (31%) are labelled `disciplined`,
above it `flexible` — 267 vs. 253, a near-even split, which is itself a
sign the median cutoff isn't picking up a degenerate distribution.

**Why rule-based, not a clustering model:** with 520 clients and a feature
set that's already mostly categorical (client_tier, campaign_frequency,
bundle_affinity, negotiation_leverage) plus the two continuous fields above,
fitting k-means/GMM on top would mean *choosing k* and then interpreting
opaque cluster centroids back into the same four-ish buckets that already
exist as ground truth in `campaign_objective`. That trade — opacity for no
accuracy gain — is exactly what CLAUDE.md's deterministic-core principle
warns against. `ClientSegmentationEngine` is a direct, auditable lookup:
every segment cites the exact booking count and share behind it.

## 2. What is *not* modelled: "ground-level footfall improvement" clients

Requested but has no data binding anywhere in `client_facts`, `bookings`,
or `lost_leads` — no column measures a client's interest in local-area
impact as distinct from the four `campaign_objective` values.
**Deliberately not built**, flagged here as a real capability gap rather
than approximated with an invented proxy.

## 3. A related finding: `negotiation_leverage` does not do what it sounds like

While looking for a second way to use `client_facts` to adjust pricing, the
obvious hypothesis was tested: does a client's `negotiation_leverage`
predict a lower realised price (i.e., can they negotiate harder)? Measured
on settled, de-duplicated deals, holding city + screen_type fixed to avoid
a mix-shift confound (LH `metro_station` only, 16,501 deals):

| negotiation_leverage | mean price/slot/day | median | n |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| high | 133.10 | 132.76 | 1,164 |
| medium | 114.95 | 108.65 | 4,354 |
| low | 107.80 | 100.01 | 10,983 |

**High-leverage clients pay *more*, not less** — the opposite of the naive
assumption. This is almost certainly confounded with client size/tier
(bigger accounts plausibly buy premium inventory and are independently
coded as higher-leverage) rather than a clean causal effect of leverage on
price. **No pricing rule was built on this column** — doing so without
first controlling for the confound (a regression holding tier, bundle size,
and cohort fixed) would risk shipping a backwards adjustment. Recorded here
as a finding for further study, not a features.
